When your power goes off, the first question to settle is whether it is just
your house or the whole street. The answer changes who you should call and what
you should tell them. The order below saves you a wait that may not be
necessary.

## The fault line

KIB-TEK's electricity fault line is **`188`**. It covers the whole island and
takes calls at any hour.

If you cannot reach it from your line, or you are calling from a foreign
number, the alternative is **`(0392) 600 09 60`**.

For the Güzelyurt region a mobile number is also published:
**`0548 888 88 84`**.

These numbers are for faults. For billing, meters, new registrations, or debt
questions, call your regional office instead — the fault line does not handle
them.

## Check three things first

These take about a minute, and they often show the problem is inside the house
rather than on the network.

1. **Look at your fuse box.** If a residual-current device or a breaker has
   tripped, only your home is dark. Switch it off and back on. If it trips
   again, one of your appliances is at fault — that is an electrician's job, not
   the utility's.
2. **Look at the neighbours.** Are the street lights on? Is the building
   opposite lit? If you are the only dark house, the problem may be in your own
   connection.
3. **Check whether work was announced.** Planned outages are announced in
   advance. The front page of this site shows what is known right now and what
   is scheduled. There is no need to report announced work.

## What to say when you call

How quickly a crew finds the fault depends on what you tell them. Have this
ready:

- **The address**: village or neighbourhood, street, building. A nearby
  landmark helps too.
- **When it went off**: a rough time is fine.
- **How wide the area is**: just your house, the street, or the whole village?
- **Anything you can see**: a fallen or hanging cable, a leaning pole, a bang, a
  burning smell, or a noise from the transformer. These details decide what
  equipment the crew brings.
- **Your account number**: it is on your bill. Not required, but it speeds
  things up.

## When it is dangerous

**Never approach a cable that has fallen or is hanging low.** You cannot tell
from looking whether a line is dead. A conductor lying on the ground can make
the ground you are standing on live as well.

Stay at least ten metres away, keep others back, and call `188` immediately. If
there are animals or children nearby, say so on the phone.

Call the same number if a pole is sparking, a transformer is smoking, or you can
smell burning, and describe what you can see.

## How long will it take?

There is no answer anyone can guarantee, and you should be sceptical of anyone
who gives you one. It depends on the kind of fault, how many households it
affects, the weather, and how many crews are out at the time.

Broadly, a fault affecting a wide area is dealt with before a single house.
That is frustrating if you are the single house, but it is the sensible order
for bringing a network back up.

What you can do while you wait is in our
[long outage guide](/en/guides/long-outage). For protecting your appliances when
the power returns, see the [surge guide](/en/guides/surge-protection).

## Should you report the same fault twice?

No. Calling repeatedly about the same fault occupies the line and does not make
anything happen faster. Once your report is logged, a crew has been assigned.

Call again only if something has **changed**: a new hazard around the cable, a
fire risk, or the power coming back and going off again. That is new
information, and the crew needs it.
